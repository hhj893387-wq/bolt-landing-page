Bolt is a free video downloader made by one person. This page explains, in plain words, what data Bolt and this website handle and what they do not.

Summary

  • Bolt has no accounts, no ads, and no tracking of what you download.
  • The app can send anonymous usage statistics to help me fix bugs and improve Bolt. You can turn this off in Settings.
  • I never collect the links you paste, video titles, or your files.
  • I do not sell or share your data.

What Bolt collects

When anonymous analytics is on, Bolt records short events about how the app is used. Each event contains:

  • A random install ID and a random session ID. They are made on your computer and are not linked to your name, email, or Windows account.
  • The event name, the Bolt version, your Windows version, and the time.
  • Event details such as: which screen or button was used, which options you picked (quality, format, convert, subtitles, scope), which features you used (trim, playlist, rename, queue), whether a download finished, failed, or was cancelled, the site name and format of a finished download, a short error category, how long a session lasted, and how long it took to start the first download.
  • Crash location if the app crashes: the error type, function name, file name, and line number only.

This helps me see which features matter, where people get stuck, and what is crashing.

What it never collects

  • The links you paste, video titles, channel names, or your download history.
  • Your downloaded files or anything in your save folder.
  • Your name, email address, phone number, or any account details.
  • Error messages, because they can contain file paths or links. Only the crash location is sent.
  • Passwords, cookies, or browsing history.

Bolt only accepts a fixed list of event types, and text values are cut short, so a bug cannot send long text such as a link or a title by accident.

How it is protected

Event details are encrypted on your computer before they are sent. Bolt only holds a public key, which can lock data but cannot unlock it. Only I hold the private key needed to read the details.

The app's connection to the database can only add new rows. It cannot read, change, or delete anything.

About IP addresses. Any internet request reveals your IP address to the server that receives it. Bolt does not put your IP address in the analytics data. The database provider may keep standard server logs as part of running their service.

Your choice

You can turn anonymous analytics off at any time in Bolt's Settings. When it is off, nothing is recorded or sent, and any events waiting on your computer are deleted.

On your device

Bolt stores a few files on your computer:

  • A settings file, which includes your preferences and your random install ID.
  • A small queue file with analytics events that have not been sent yet (for example, when you are offline). It is deleted after sending or when you turn analytics off.
  • The videos and audio you download, in the folder you choose. These never leave your computer.

Uninstalling Bolt and deleting its data folder removes all of this.

Third parties

I do not sell your data and I do not share it for advertising. The only service involved is Supabase, which stores the encrypted analytics events.

When you download a video, Bolt connects directly from your computer to the site that hosts it. That site has its own privacy policy.

Deleting your data

Analytics data is tied only to a random install ID, so I cannot find it by your name or email. If you want your analytics data deleted, email me your install ID (it is stored in Bolt's settings file) and I will delete the rows linked to it. You can also turn analytics off at any time.

Contact

Questions about privacy? Email support@bytebolt.app. It is a one-person project, so every message is read.
